# 3 directions dealt for this brief

Choose the one whose way of composing can best carry an idea from your inventory (SKILL.md steps 1 and 4), not the
most familiar one. Then deal it in full with the same command plus `--direction <id>` (and without --options).

## `page-is-the-object` — The page is the object
Idea engine: Find the printed or physical artefact that already exists in the subject's world and build the page as that thing. A wedding site is the invitation card.
Composition: The first screen shows the artefact as a single filled panel (paper colour on a contrasting ground) centred or offset, with believable proportions: a card at 5:7, a ticket at about 2.2:1 with a notch cut by `clip-path`, a menu as a tall sheet. All primary content lives on it, set the way that object is set: centred and symmetrical for an invitation, in tidy aligned columns for a menu or ticket.

## `split-stage` — The split stage
Idea engine: Half the screen never moves. One side is a fixed photograph (or object, or product view) that stays in place for the whole visit; the other side is a calm scrolling column with everything you need to read.
Composition: Desktop: a two-column page, 50/50 or 45/55. One column is `position: sticky; top: 0; height: 100svh` and holds a single photograph filling it edge to edge, or the object centred on a flat field.

## `specimen-catalogue` — The specimen catalogue
Idea engine: Present what is on offer the way a field guide presents birds: each item alone on a flat colour, same scale, same angle, named plainly. A bakery's pastries become specimens and the specimens become the menu.
Composition: Hero: a left-aligned statement of what this is (5–7vw, upper-left) beside or above a tight cluster of four to seven specimen tiles of different sizes arranged as a loose mosaic that runs off the right edge. Each tile is a flat colour square or 4:5 rectangle with one photograph centred on it, no border, 8–12px gaps.
